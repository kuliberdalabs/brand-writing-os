# Channel patterns

Use these as decision guides, not fixed templates.

## Blog article

- State the practical value early.
- Give the reader enough context to judge whether the advice fits.
- Build sections around questions or decisions, not arbitrary word counts.
- Use evidence where the claim appears.
- End on the last useful implication or next step; avoid a ceremonial recap.

## Thought leadership and LinkedIn

- Start from one sourced observation, decision, result, or tension.
- Keep one controlling idea.
- Make the speaker's judgment visible, including a tradeoff or limit.
- Avoid manufactured controversy and engagement bait unless the profile explicitly allows it.
- Use a CTA only when it follows from the point.

## Newsletter

- Make the reason to read clear near the top.
- Maintain a recognizable sender perspective.
- Separate editorial content from an offer.
- Keep links, sponsorships, and claims accurate at send time.

## Landing page

- Match the reader's problem to an approved promise.
- Explain the mechanism and boundary of the offer.
- Use only approved proof.
- Resolve objections with concrete information.
- Treat pricing, guarantees, and availability as live data that requires a current source.

## Case study

- Confirm permission to name the client.
- Separate starting condition, intervention, and measured result.
- Preserve time period, baseline, attribution, and limitations.
- Use direct quotations only when approved verbatim.
- Do not imply that one result is typical unless evidence supports it.

## Email

- Use the relationship and context actually available.
- Do not invent familiarity, shared history, urgency, or scarcity.
- Keep the ask proportionate to the relationship.
- Sending remains a separate, explicit action.
